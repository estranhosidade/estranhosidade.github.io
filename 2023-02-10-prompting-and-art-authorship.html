Prompting and Art Authorship

10/02/2023 estranhosidade Deixe um comentário

A somewhat lengthy text that I would like to share. There has been a lot of debate regarding AI art, the main questions on the issues being raised – aside from economics – as far as I could see are essentially: Is AI art… art? And if so, who is the artist? The algorithm, the human, both? Nobody? I have been thinking on that and I would like to give my brief two cents:

1) Is “AI Art”… Art?
This one is pretty simple as well: at least in principle, yes. To be clear, now I’m just referring to the art in itself, the issue whether the prompt writer is or isn’t an artist I will address later on. With that said:

Get your favourite piece of art, maybe your favourite movie or TV show or music, it doesn’t matter. Now imagine for a moment that it was actually created by a machine and not a human being. The art is still exactly the same, with the only difference being the process, imagine it was actually all created entirely in a computer by futuristic algorithms. Would you stop appreciating that? Would you denounce that art and call it cheap? And say it stopped touching you? Would you put that away and never look back?

I honestly think most people wouldn’t. You see, many try to argue that AI art is not art because “oh, there is no author intent”. Well, first there is intent, there is the intent of the person who wrote the prompt and curated that job until you end up with that given version – much like photography demonstrates, curating plays a major role on the artistic expression. But even if that wasn’t the case and those prompts had themselves been generated by the algorithm… it doesn’t matter.

It is less about the author and more about: can people relate to that? Call that thing touching and moving? If so… it’s weird to say it is not art just because it wasn’t created by a human. It seems a cynical view to be like “Oh, before I decide whether I love that art I have to find out who created this piece of work!”.

Art is not only about the author or the process – only you see your process, and, hell, many people don’t have any idea about the authors who created the art they love – it is about that public as well and how they will perceive that media. Not coincidentally, you oftentimes have pieces of art that the public interpretation totally differs from the author’s one. People who point out that “lack of intent” on AI art disregard this as well, essentially artists overestimate how much of a role their intent plays on the public perception of the art they create, as well as its importance, but art speaks for itself.

And, even if you totally disregard the prompt writer intention and only focus on the painter/photographer intentions, arguing there is no intent on the drawings created by a machine: It is worth to highlight that those datasets used to train DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney and others, those datasets are not based on some alien and foreign world history that is totally unrelatable to humans, on the contrary. A huge chunk of human knowledge and history is there, so whatever those algorithms put out, they are created based on humanity. There was an intent behind them, the machine would still be reproducing such intent and human expression one way or the other.

With that said: at least theoretically, AI art can be art. It doesn’t mean “all AI art is art” necessarily, though. In the same way that most people wouldn’t say all photographs, drawings and paintings are art (although there are those who disagree and would advocate for anything – even a fountain in a museum – being art).

To put it simply: You have content that most people would call “cheap soulless disposable mass manufactured” being produced by humans, and you have content that most people would call “cheap soulless disposable mass manufactured” being produced by AIs as well. Whether you consider those art or not: the criteria should be the same for both.

2) Are “prompt engineers” artists?

A more humble, way of putting this question is: do people giving the overall idea of an art can be considered an artist? This one is pretty simple as well: at least in principle, yes.

Hell, there is a whole art category to define people who make art writing words, we call them writers. It’s worth to highlight and to point out that, whenever you talk about art, you have two aspects of artistic creation, you have the overall idea and you have that technical execution of that given art form (painting, drawing, etc…), bringing this to music, for instance, you could compare coming up with the lyrics of a song versus the harmony and singing.

Or, to give an even better example: you have art directors and graphical illustrators. A person can be a master regarding the technical aspect of painting, their brush techniques, their Photoshop skills, and what have you, but have very little creativity on what to paint and how to communicate a message or a feeling.

With the invention of these algorithms, what happens is that those two roles are essentially fragmented, and access to art skills become accessible to anyone. Essentially, art styles themselves become a tool. You don’t need the technical knowledge/technique to transmit a message anymore, but there is still a message to be transmitted.

I think a lot of the outrage of artists is because they keep comparing prompt engineers with painters, which they are not. In the same way, a painter making a hyperrealistic painting which is indistinguishable from a photo isn’t a photographer, even if the result they arrive is the same. But they are both artist.

There are skills to be learned. You have to tell it what you want: What art style you would like? What about the lightning? The angle? The lens? The composition? You have to have skills, because the machine can’t plug into your mind and read your thoughts. Therefore, prompting differs, but it can be comparable to photography: you are photographing latent spaces with words.

To get back to my initial point, if you have a machine that is able to generate a sequence of Forrest Gump and you press a button and do nothing more. That thing the machine created is art, but, I believe, there was not enough involvement of your part to say that such thing is your creation.

Now put the following scenario, you wrote an idea of this story, you wrote a 50-page script giving a general path you wanted the story to take, you choose a director that you would want to direct your movie, you have a deeper involvement in that creation, much like a movie director.

So it depends on your involvement in that given process. Did you just type “Space girl trending on art station” or did you go further? Generating countless versions, refining, adjusting and tweaking every minor detail until you got what you wanted? The more input you give, the more you can say you created something, I think the main issue here is that there is yet no rule of thumb/established criteria to decide the “general level of user input” required (if any?) to prompt writers being considered artists, since this art form has only existed and became mainstream for a few months.

4) Final considerations and the future of this technology:

“There’s more art in my head—Joe Ordinary—than there is in half the museums on this planet. Well how, then—what’s maddening is how narrow the reduction valve is. You know, we’ve been making art for 5,000 years and what have we got? We’ve got a few museums full of some nice stuff. But what have we got in our heads? 50,000 times more good stuff, but very hard to get out. And really hard to get out when you’re carving it in diorite and granite.” – Terence McKenna.

This technology will keep evolving in the years to come, it will compose music, it will write books, and it will eventually create full length movies, which will be indistinguishable from the ones created by humans. Ultimately, what AI art will achieve is to allow any art that anyone ever wished it existed… to actually exist.

What needs to be explained here is a large chunk of art production, especially TV shows, movies and animes are subjected to market pressures, which deeply limits the existence of such content. To put simply : unless such piece of art is being created by a millionaire, or a dictator with a fervent passion for art, that thing needs to adapt to market pressure, otherwise it can’t be done, it can’t exist (or you have to compromise so much of your artistic vision that the final result is a shallow of what you had in mind.)

Therefore, the art that exist is only a tiny fraction of the art people would want to see, enough to appeal to the biggest possible demographic that would consume such content, so that studios can make their investment back. Commercial art is not only about art, but is much about money as well. No studio would invest 10 million dollars on an anime that is only watched by 2000 people in the world.

Other medias where the production costs are cheaper, such as books, represent better what people wanted to see. In the sense that the books that exist are probably a more precise representation from the books that people wanted to read, that anyone, any small demographic, would want to read, because anyone can write a book. The price is much cheaper. And essentially, this is what AI art will make it, AI Art will free art from economic restrictions and skill limitations.

This text might or might have not been written by a machine running GPT-3.
