Philosophical Zombies, Consciousness and Artificial Intelligence

30/06/2016 estranhosidade Deixe um comentário


I was thinking a little bit about the two forms of a computer to perform a human task, and by “human” here I mean things like learning how to perform image recognition, to play chess, to translate texts, etc, etc…

Today we do this using things like learning algorithms and so on, which is a very different way of how human mind perform such tasks.

Or, of course, you can also teach the assumptions of that particular field of knowledge to the machine, just as IBM taught Deep Blue to play chess.

I believe that the point where I wanna get here is the fact that there are tasks that require assumptions that until today have proven themselves too complex to teach machines to understand. And the point is: would the only way for us to achieve these goals be completely simulating a human mind?

Or could we also achieve this through the approach of using a series of isolated technologies? That is, instead you simulating an entire human mind to translate a video, you would use an algorithm to transcribe what was said, another algorithm to translate, another algorithm to analyze possible cultural references mentioned and adapt them, and so on?

In other words, you would have a series of algorithms that would try to get the same result of a human mind, however, addressing the situation in a completely different way.

There is an episode of the series from the English channel, Channel 4, and of Netflix also, called “Black Mirror”, a fantastic series by the way, that address a bit this issue. For those who don’t know, briefly, the series shows the “dark” aspects of new technologies.

And, one of its episodes, actually a Christmas special, tells a story in which, basically, people in the world, in order to have a perfect virtual assistant, did a mind copying (also known as mind uploading, if you prefer to call it that) of their minds.

Creating, for all we know, a sentient version of them which was a sort of slave. In short, people cloned their minds and the digital copy was in charge, or more precisely saying, condemned to be an eternal assistant in their daily life.

If they refused, they, the copies, were threatened with the horror of being in a dark room for many mouths (since time could be speeded up for them, as a form of torture if they did not give in). By the way, it’s eerily fascinating this dehumanization of an apparently sentient artificial intelligence demonstrated in the episode, a rational being is treated as just one software being analyzed for bugs.

Of course, I don’t think that this would be so tragic and macabre in real life since, even if we needed a sentient intelligence as a requirement for them to be a strong artificial intelligence, we could, for example, delete the parts of the mind and personality of this AI that would get tired or that would rebel against serving us, we could performed a kind of “digital lobotomy” on it. Obviously, I’m saying this based on the premise that it would be “sentient” on some level.

Which brings us to a very curious and profound philosophical question:

“How can we do something that acts in a way completely indistinguishable from a human, that can do all human tasks, including to prove its humanity… but who is not sentient? Or is it necessary, as a logical premise, to the operation of a system that simulates human behavior in an indistinguishable way, that that system in question ‘necessarily had an awareness of their existence’?”

I mean, this goes into that old discussion regarding solipsism and philosophical zombies. This makes us think about things like: is there a part of the human mind responsible for our “sense of awareness of our own existence”, and another part responsible for doing human tasks? Or, for example, by creating a strong artificial intelligence, would we necessarily be creating a sentient intelligence?

I mean, due the fact that we only have knowledge of one “intelligent” species, ourselves, we ended up not knowing whether consciousness is a logical and inherently necessary premise for an intelligence like ours, or if we could do an intelligence that produces a similar result to the one of a human, or even an indistinguishable one, but which “didn’t realize the fact that it really exists”.

Or maybe we could do both, who knows. Maybe we could create these 4 different types of AI:

• Sentient Artificial Intelligence Emancipated: These would be totally autonomous and unpredictable, similar to us humans. You wouldn’t be able to simply control them, or you would have as much trouble to enslave them as you would have to enslave a human. They would be, as the name implies, sentient, they would have self-awareness of their existence, instead of being just philosophical zombies.

• Sentient Artificial Intelligence Lobotomized: These would be fully sentient beings, but they would have been “brainwashed” or would have had an editing in their software to always obey us and to never rebel. We can compare them with people who go through some trauma, such as the Stockholm syndrome, and after that begin to have a distorted view of reality. A lobotomized AI would be as aware, and have as much self-awareness of their own existence, as a human who had gone through this kind of mental manipulation. And, it seems, this is precisely the kind of AI which is shown in that particular episode of the series mentioned above.

And finally, we have 2 variations of the AI types mentioned above:

• Philosophical Zombie Artificial Intelligence Emancipated: These would be an AI exactly as the first mentioned; they would have an apparent desire to be free, you would struggle to enslave them and so on. Anyway, we can imagine that this intelligence would be just like a human… but it wouldn’t really have an awareness of their existence. It would just be a computer interacting with information, pretending to be aware of their existence. However, in practical terms, it would be indistinguishable from the first type mentioned.

• Philosophical Zombie Artificial Intelligence Lobotomized: Probably the most ethical (and safe for mankind) of all the types mentioned, this would be an AI that would not be aware of the very fact of its existence, and deep inside it would be just an actor. And it would have been modified to just have the behaviors that we wished, so that they have no desire to be free and always obey us, etc…

I believe that there will always be some doubt about the existence of the sentience of an artificial intelligence, especially a “Philosophical Zombie Artificial Intelligence Emancipated”. Even if there were some algorithm that we could point and say “look, this is the algorithm, or piece of hardware, that makes a being conscious of their own existence”… how would we know if such algorithm is working?

It is impossible to separate the idea of “being conscious and manifest your conscience” to the idea of “acting as if you were aware, however, not being really conscious”, because the end result of both premises would be the same. The demonstration of consciousness, or the apparent demonstration of consciousness, is the same.

So it would be hard to prove… as hard as it is for others to prove us that they actually exist, and that “we” are not just a brain in a jar receiving electrical stimulation. The reality is experienced only in first-person, and a future perspective of an apparent machine awareness makes this whole matter even more profound and complex.

This is why I think it’s important to study the field of ethics in relation to robots, known as “Roboethics”. It’s important that we build robots so that we know that we don’t have moral obligations to them. It would be cruel if our tools were aware and we force them to be our eternal slaves.

And, just to finish, a question that I believe we all should ask ourselves is: would we be willing to create lobotomized artificial intelligence to be our slaves, if we are not absolutely sure of their non-sentience?
